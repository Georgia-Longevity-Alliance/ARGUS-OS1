Dear Dr Basto,

I've been following your work on centriole biology and centrosome amplification. I'm writing because I think there is a natural overlap with a project we are building.

We are building ARGUS — an automated microscope that tracks individual centrioles through the full C. elegans embryogenesis, from zygote to about a hundred cells. The idea is to test whether the pattern of cell divisions — what we call Pedigree Score — predicts which centrioles a cell keeps and which it eliminates. About 88% of centrioles are gone by the comma stage, but nobody has tracked them cell by cell through the whole embryo.

The platform is OpenFlexure-based, with a Jetson Orin running CellPose for segmentation and a custom tracker for centrioles across frames. The first build is almost done. We have a Foresight Institute proposal submitted — deadline this week — and are planning ERC and EIC applications for the full experiment.

I'm looking for a collaborator who knows centrioles deeply — someone who can help make sure the biology is right. I'm not asking for funding or resources. Just a conversation.

If you're open to a brief chat, I'd be grateful.
