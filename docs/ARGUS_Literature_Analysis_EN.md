## 0. Method and coverage

- Search: **Europe PMC** (covers Medline/PubMed + PubMed Central full text + preprints), ~20 targeted and thematic queries per element, executed 2026-10-21.
- **Honest scope statement:** this is the core biomedical index (PubMed/Medline + PMC). Full breadth (Web of Science, Scopus, OpenAlex, Google Scholar) would require additional access; this document reports what is confirmable from the publicly indexed core.
- **Knowledge-credit principle:** (1) every claim carries PMID/DOI; (2) fact ≠ hypothesis; (3) counter-arguments and weak spots are stated explicitly; (4) «not found» ≠ «does not exist» — recorded as a boundary of the conclusion.

## 1. Terminology (fixed)

- **Centriole — an organelle** (a conserved microtubule-based cylindrical organelle, ~200–250 nm; per Nigg & Holland 2018, «centrioles are conserved microtubule-based organelles»).
- **Centrosome — an ultrastructure / microtubule-organizing centre** = two centrioles (mother + daughter) + pericentriolar material (PCM).
- «Centrosome age» in the literature means the **age asymmetry of the two centrioles within it** (semiconservative duplication: the mother centriole is retained, the daughter is newly formed). In this document and in the ARGUS protocol, age refers to the **centriole (the organelle)**.

## 2. Element by element — what has actually been done

### E.1 Hayflick limit in immortalized (hTERT) fibroblasts

| Claim | Status | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| hTERT introduced into normal human cells (BJ fibroblasts, RPE); clones exceeded the limit by ≥ 20 doublings, normal karyotype, no senescence; causal link between telomere shortening and replicative senescence | ✅ established | Bodnar et al., Science 1998. PMID 9454332, DOI 10.1126/science.279.5349.349 |
| hTERT-immortalized fibroblasts divide «indefinitely» in standard culture (common laboratory practice) | ✅ established (practice) | review: Shitova, Alpeeva, Vorotelyak, IJMS 2024. PMID 39684765, DOI 10.3390/ijms252313054 |
| Assessment of hTERT «immortality» has no single standard; studies evaluate different parameters | ✅ established (methodological uncertainty) | PMID 39684765 |
| **Whether BJ-hTERT/MRC5-hTERT have a HIDDEN replicative limit under long-term clonal observation of single lines (not population)** | ❓ **no direct experiment — novelty** | search 2026-10-21; no long-term clonal test found |
| Telomere-independent senescence exists (p16/pRb, oxidative stress); not all aging paths are closed by hTERT | ✅ established | senescence literature (see Section 5) |
| Telomere crisis is a tumor-suppressive barrier; rare cells escape elimination | ✅ established | Nassour & Karlseder, CSH Perspect Med 2025. PMID 40789644, DOI 10.1101/cshperspect.a041688 |

**E.1 conclusion.** Bypass of the telomeric limit by hTERT is fact. But «immortality» as a verified status of single hTERT lines over long-term observation is a **methodologically open question** (Shitova 2024), and no direct clonal test for a hidden limit exists. The fundamental question is **novel and testable**; the classical view (telomerase = immortality) is the counter-position that must be explicitly falsified.

### E.2 Centriole age → cell behaviour/fate (human)

| Claim | Status | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| **Centriole age breaks spindle symmetry even in «symmetric» divisions** of human epithelial and FIBROBLASTIC lines → unequal daughter sizes; mechanism: Odf2/cenexin-bound Plk1; the old centriole with higher MT nucleation (pericentrin, γ-tubulin, TPX2, ch-TOG) | ✅ established (direct experiment, human culture) | Thomas & Meraldi, J Cell Biol 2024. PMID 39012627, DOI 10.1083/jcb.202311153 |
| Centriole age correlates with asymmetric fate: the daughter with the daughter (younger mother) centriole receives more CD133⁺ recycling endosomes (stemness); Odf2 is the key | ✅ established (SK-N-DZ, human neuroblastoma) | Izumi, Mae, Tsukita, Nakagawara, iScience 2026. PMID 42602968, DOI 10.1016/j.isci.2026.117062 |
| The mother centriole remains at the niche → stem-cell self-renewal | ✅ established (Drosophila GSC) | Yamashita et al., Science 2007. PMID 17255513, DOI 10.1126/science.1134910 |
| Asymmetric centrosome inheritance maintains neural-progenitor self-renewal (mouse) | ✅ established | Wang, Tsai, Shi et al., Nature 2009. PMID 19829375, DOI 10.1038/nature08435 |
| Centrosome asymmetry (PCM1) + polarized endosomes → daughter fate | ✅ established (human) | Zhao, Royer, et al., Nat Commun 2025. PMID 41315244, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-65756-2 |
| **Division-rate dynamics (interdivision time) OLD vs NEW over MONTHS** | ❓ **no direct experiment — novelty** | only point effects found (spindle, fate, endosomes); no long-term OLD/NEW pair dynamics |

**E.2 conclusion.** The effect of **centriole age** on division and fate in human cells is **confirmed** — a strong external anchor for ARGUS. ARGUS's novelty is precisely the **long-term dynamics** (months) of division rate of a single OLD/NEW pair, which does not exist in the literature.

### E.3 Dispersion/degradation of the oldest centriole (centriole as counter)

| Claim | Status | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Selective accumulation of old centrioles in stem cells over time (theoretical work) | 🟡 theory/proposal (J. Tqemaladze) | Tkemaladze J., Mol Biol Rep 2022. PMID 36583780, DOI 10.1007/s11033-022-08203-5 |
| **Direct experiment «centriole dispersion masks the Hayflick limit»** | ❓ **not performed — novelty** | search 2026-10-21 |
| Centriole structural-integrity defects are associated with syndromes (hydrolethalus et al.) | ✅ established | Curinha, Holland, et al., J Cell Biol 2025. PMID 40009365, DOI 10.1083/jcb.202403022 |
| Tubulin code / post-translational modifications — markers of centrosome/centriole state (potential age markers) | ✅ established (markers); ❓ age-specificity partially | Wang C. et al., Cytoskeleton 2026. PMID 41174898, DOI 10.1002/cm.70062; Vilela et al., Cancer Metastasis Rev 2026. PMID 42257920 |
| Centrosome/cilium/centriolar satellite axis in aging and neurodegeneration | ✅ actively studied | Sahin & Firat-Karalar, EMBO Rep 2026. PMID 42481875, DOI 10.1038/s44319-026-00877-3 |

**E.3 conclusion.** The mechanism itself («centriolar dispersion → counter failure → hidden limit») is a **hypothesis with no direct experiment** — high novelty. The literature supplies markers (tubulin PTMs, Odf2, Cep164) and frameworks. **Weak spot (must be addressed):** Thomas & Meraldi (E.2) show that in normal cells the old mother centriole retains/increases nucleation capacity — «old» ≠ «degraded» on short windows. The dispersion hypothesis requires the degradation to manifest at the scale of **hundreds of divisions** — this is the scale discriminator that ARGUS must test.

### E.4 Supernumerary centrioles in cancer

| Claim | Status | Source |
|-------|--------|--------|
| Centriole duplication «once and only once»; deregulation → cancer, neurodevelopment | ✅ established | Nigg & Holland, Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol 2018. PMID 29363672, DOI 10.1038/nrm.2017.127 |
| Supernumerary centrosomes → p53 activation (PIDDosome) — the cell limits proliferation | ✅ established | Fava et al., Genes Dev 2017. PMID 28130345, DOI 10.1101/gad.289728.116 |
| Centriole amplification in early B-cell development — but dispensable | ✅ established | Schapfl, Holland, et al., Nat Commun 2024. PMID 39406735, DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-53222-4 |
| Structural centrosome aberrations → invasiveness, non-cell-autonomous effects | ✅ established | Ganier, Nigg, et al., EMBO J 2018. PMID 29567643, DOI 10.15252/embj.201798576 |
| **«Young new centrioles beside old ones in the same cell postpone/mask the Hayflick limit»** | ❓ **no direct experiment — novelty** | search 2026-10-21 |

**E.4 conclusion.** The fact of supernumerary centrioles in cancer and their effect on proliferation is **confirmed**. But the literature sees amplification chiefly as a **source of instability/death** (p53 pathway, Fava 2017), not as a «counter reset to immortality». **Weak spot of the hypothesis:** amplified cells often proliferate worse / die more — «young centrioles confer immortality» may contradict the data; the cancer part therefore needs explicit proliferation/survival controls.

## 3. Meta-analysis and weak spots (summary)

**Strongly confirmed by the literature (anchors for the projects):**
1. hTERT bypass of the telomeric limit is fact (Bodnar 1998); but the «immortality» status of single lines is methodologically unsettled (Shitova 2024).
2. Centriole **age** affects division/fate in human cells: spindle/daughter size (Thomas & Meraldi 2024), endosomal fate/stemness (Izumi 2026), niche (Yamashita 2007), neural progenitors (Wang 2009), PCM1 (Zhao 2025).
3. Centriole-duplication control and its deregulation in cancer (Nigg & Holland 2018; Fava 2017).

**Novelty of ARGUS (not performed by anyone):**
- Hidden replicative limit in immortalized (hTERT) fibroblasts (E.1) — not directly tested.
- Long-term (months) OLD/NEW division-rate dynamics (E.2) — no long-term measurement exists.
- Centriolar dispersion as limit masking (E.3) — hypothesis without experiment.
- Young supernumerary centrioles as a «limit postponer» (E.4) — hypothesis without experiment.
- 24/7 months single-cell observation regime (E.5/E.6) — unique.

**Weak spots / counter-arguments (must be built into the design):**
1. **«Old = nucleation advantage» (Thomas & Meraldi 2024)** — in normal cells the old mother centriole is not «worn out» but retains high nucleation capacity. The dispersion hypothesis requires degradation at the scale of hundreds of divisions. → Record a **scale discriminator** in the protocol, with controls (tubulin-PTM markers, Odf2, Cep164; EM at endpoint).
2. **p53 paradox of amplification (Fava 2017)** — supernumerary centrioles activate p53 and limit proliferation; «young centrioles = immortality» may contradict the data. → The cancer part needs mandatory survival/proliferation controls.
3. **Observation horizon** — expected limit in immortalized cells ≈ 800–1000 divisions: at ~1 division per 20–30 h this is ~2–3 years of pure dynamics. → «Not detected in the window» ≠ «no limit»; record the boundary and, if needed, add conditions that load the centriole faster.
4. **Daughter-size asymmetry (Thomas & Meraldi)** — centriole age itself produces daughter asymmetry; when measuring «OLD vs NEW division rate», cause (centriole age) must not be confused with consequence (daughter size → next division). → Include size/fate as covariates.
5. **Phototoxicity** — any long-term regime itself alters dynamics; need control V6 (proliferation in chamber vs plate) and «image on demand» mode.
6. **Terminology** — strictly «centriole (organelle)», «centrosome (ultrastructure)»; age refers to the centriole.

## 4. Conclusions for the projects

**For ARGUS:**
- The experiment is **novel** in every key element (hidden limit, long-term OLD/NEW dynamics, observation regime), while its components are **supported** by the literature (E.2 especially).
- Strengthen the design: scale discriminator (E.3), p53/proliferation controls for the cancer part (E.4), size/fate covariates, observation horizon with a stated boundary.
- The 24/7/months regime is a unique niche — a strong novelty argument for the grant and the publication.

**For CDATA/CEDAR (centriole as a division and aging counter):**
- External anchors: Thomas & Meraldi 2024 (centriole age → division, human), Izumi 2026 (centriole age → fate, human), Nigg & Holland 2018 (duplication), Curinha 2025 (centriole integrity).
- Age/state markers of the centriole: Odf2/cenexin, Cep164, tubulin PTMs (glutamylation/polyglycylation) — use as independent confirmation in the protocol.

**For the grant application (ARGUS-OS1 / OSC):**
- Frame elements as «element confirmed by literature (PMID)» + «long-term OLD/NEW dynamics — novel, not measured by anyone». State the gaps and counter-arguments honestly — this raises scientific credibility rather than lowering it.
