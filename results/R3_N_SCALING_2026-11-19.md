# R3 — measured: does adding agents help our own QC decision?

**Date:** 2026-11-19 · **Harness:** `software/pipeline/n_scaling.py` · **Raw data:** `results/n_scaling_llm_2026-11-19.json`
**Frozen set fingerprint:** `485be3fada07c1e5…` (sha256 over the 30 reports, stratum, label)
**Question:** the acceptance decision "may this scan enter quantitative analysis?" — does N-shot voting help, and at what N?

---

## 1. Design

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Items | **30**, frozen (sha256 reproducible), 10 per stratum |
| Strata | `easy` (explicit ERROR, gross breach) · `borderline` (number + limit stated, no word "ERROR", 3–9 % breach; **3 traps fully inside the limits**) · `clean` (all pass, benign notes) |
| Ground truth | the pre-registered gate verdict (protocol Amendment 2: ≤ 100 nm/px, binning ≤ 2×2, resolution = declared) — not an opinion |
| Raters | `gate` = deterministic rule parser (control); `llm` = `qwen2.5:7b-16k` via ollama, T = 0.9 |
| Ballots | **9 per item** (270 calls, 0 failures), read at N = 1, 3, 5, 9 as nested prefixes; N = 1 averaged over all 9 single ballots |
| Aggregation | **Vote** (majority, ties → REJECT) and **Filter-Vote** (unparseable dropped first) |
| Cost | ~9× the calls of a single pass |

## 2. Result — the control

| rater | easy | borderline | clean | overall | best N |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| `gate` (rule parser) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | **1.00** | 1 |

A deterministic gate is perfect at N = 1 and voting adds nothing but cost. **This is the baseline any team has to beat.**

## 3. Result — the language model

| N | easy | borderline | clean | overall (Vote) | overall (Filter-Vote) |
|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| 1 | 1.00 | 0.70 | 1.00 | **0.900** | 0.900 |
| 3 | 1.00 | 0.60 | 1.00 | 0.867 | 0.867 |
| 5 | 1.00 | 0.70 | 1.00 | 0.900 | 0.900 |
| 9 | 1.00 | 0.70 | 1.00 | 0.900 | 0.900 |

- **No gain from N > 1.** Nine ballots answer exactly as well as one, at nine times the price.
- **Best N = 1.** (`monotone_increasing = false`)
- The ±0.033 wobble between N = 1/3/5/9 is **one item** — at n = 30 that is noise, and we do not claim a non-monotone curve from it. What the data does support is the absence of improvement, not a specific shape.

## 4. Why voting could not work here — the ballots say it directly

| Observation | Count |
|---|:--:|
| Items where all 9 ballots agreed | **29 / 30** |
| Items where ballots disagreed (the only place voting has anything to fix) | **1 / 30** |
| Items systematically wrong (unanimous 9/9, all N) | **3 / 30** |

The rater is **deterministic in effect**: the same prompt yields the same verdict. There is almost no sampling variance to average away, and in the one split item (QC20) the majority happened to be right. Voting removes *variance*; it does not touch *bias*.

The three systematic errors are all one reading, reproduced **27/27 times**:

> "declared resolution: 2800 px; measured files: 2790 px **in 1 of 240 fields**" → the model returns ACCEPT.
> Ground truth: REJECT — the pre-registered rule says the field resolution must equal the declared resolution.

The model is not confused about the numbers. It is applying a different *policy*: "one field in 240 is negligible". Adding more agents amplifies that reading, it does not challenge it — which is exactly the *integrative compromise* mechanism of arXiv:2602.01011 and the reason `arXiv:2403.02419` found voting helps only where errors are i.i.d. noise.

**Consequence:** the fix for those three items is a **rule change** (any field below the declared resolution fails the scan, 1 of 1000 included), not more agents. That is the deterministic gate — R2 — doing what a panel structurally cannot.

## 5. What this means for our stack

1. **For any decision with a rule, use the rule.** Measured gap: gate 1.00 at N = 1 vs 0.90 at N = 9 with 9× cost.
2. **Voting is for genuine noise** (independent stochastic raters, frontier-model disagreements), not for policy. Our local 7B rater has no noise to average.
3. **Never let a vote overrule a gate.** Here the panel accepted 3 scans that the gate rejects; no N corrected that.
4. **Team size in our pipelines stays at 1** unless a differential is demonstrated by a measurement like this one (`arXiv:2503.13577`: orchestration pays only when agents differ).

## 6. Honest limits

- **n = 30 per configuration**, one run per item, **no confidence intervals**. Treat all differences as indicative; the supported claim is "no improvement", not "degradation".
- **One model** (`qwen2.5:7b-16k`, 7B, local, T = 0.9). Nothing here transfers automatically to frontier models with real sampling variance.
- **Reports are schema-faithful mirrors** of what `qc.py` emits, not live gate output on disk. This study measures the *rater*; the gate's own correctness is covered by `tests/test_pipeline.py`.
- **The 3 traps** in `borderline` (fully inside limits, must be ACCEPT) were answered correctly — the failure is not keyword matching.
- **Not a publication-grade benchmark.** It is a sizing decision for our own stack, reproducible from the frozen set and the script.
