Subject: ARGUS — tracking centriole fate through C. elegans embryogenesis

Dear Dr Bettencourt-Dias,

I've read your work on centriole biogenesis and elimination for years — your 2005 PLK4 paper was one of the papers that got me into this field. I'm writing because there is a project I think you'd find interesting.

We're building ARGUS — an automated microscope that tracks individual centrioles through C. elegans embryogenesis, from the single-cell zygote through about a hundred cells. The question is simple: does the pattern of cell divisions predict which centrioles survive and which are eliminated? Kalbfuss and Gönczy showed that 88% of centrioles are gone by the comma stage, but nobody has tracked the process cell by cell, centriole by centriole, through the whole embryo.

Your 2016 Science paper on centrosome elimination in Drosophila — the mechanism you described, Polo kinase and PCM shedding — is exactly the kind of biology our platform is designed to capture in real time. ARGUS tracks both the centriole core and the PCM, so we can see the disassembly sequence.

The platform is OpenFlexure-based, with local AI on a Jetson Orin. The first build is nearly complete. We have a Foresight Institute grant under review and are preparing ERC and EIC applications.

I'm looking for a collaborator who understands centriole biology at the deepest level. Not funding, not resources — just someone who can look at the data and say whether the biology makes sense. If you're open to a conversation, I'd be very glad.
